# Findbar × FlowGastronomie

A real website improvement case study

31 October 2026

## Result

Findbar detected repeated browser titles and search descriptions on FlowGastronomie’s Albanian website. The owner reviewed concrete proposals. The developer applied six texts in the existing project, verified a private preview, and published after explicit approval. A fresh public Findbar check confirmed the result.

**3 pages · 6 changed fields · 10 pages checked again · 0 confirmed findings after publication.**

## What the case covers

The target pages were `/sq`, `/sq/pricing` and `/sq/demo/radar` on https://123serve.de. The six findings formed two grouped topics: a repeated browser title on three pages and a repeated search description on the same three pages. The large visible headings inside the pages were outside these changes.

## The complete workflow

1. **Check – 30 October, 23:29 Berlin.** The normal public website check inspected ten pages and saved six evidence-backed field findings.
2. **Understand.** Findbar grouped the findings and showed the stored text beside a proposal. The UI clarified the location: browser tab and search description.
3. **Review proposals.** The owner could read, copy and decide on the texts. Proposals used confirmed page content.
4. **Implement in the existing Flow project.** The developer added page-specific Next.js metadata. Large headings and visible page content stayed the same.
5. **Verify the private preview – 31 October.** All six requested values matched and the repetitions disappeared. These results remain explicitly private previews.
6. **Verify the release.** CI and Operations Lab passed on the final source. Stale test expectations were repaired; real provider evidence stayed separate from synthetic fixtures.
7. **Publish with approval – 31 October, 18:53 Berlin.** The developer replaced only API and web with verified images. Backup and prior images remained available.
8. **Check the real website again – 18:54 Berlin.** The normal Findbar worker inspected ten public URLs. Result: zero confirmed findings; six direct HTTPS field comparisons matched.
9. **Retain the history.** Earlier reports, proposals, approvals and private checks remain accessible beside the new public result.

## What each product contributes

**Findbar:** a confirmed business profile, bounded public website checks, grouped findings with evidence, a clear text comparison, versioned proposals/approvals, controlled private preview, history and DE/EN/SQ reports with PDF and email drafts.

**FlowGastronomie:** the real restaurant-software website and the existing application project where the developer implemented, tested and published the proposed metadata with approval.

Publication in this case was an explicitly authorised developer release. The private preview connector does not provide general autonomous production access to client websites.

## Verified quality and limits

Sixteen release gates and Operations Lab passed. Local browser tests passed 59/59. Original records across 21 Findbar tables, the encrypted key and the unchanged first export passed preservation checks. This correction cycle used no new paid AI measurement.

The profile’s 100% refers to 30 of 30 basic checks on six eligible public pages. The audit visited ten URLs overall. This result does not establish search rankings, AI recommendations, indexing or Core Web Vitals. Findbar remains a local application awaiting final OWNER_UAT; Flow fiscal LIVE and device acceptance remain separate approvals.
